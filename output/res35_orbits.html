What are "resolves" and these limits?

An ITU resolution's numbered decisions are called resolves 1, 2, 3… Resolution 8 (WRC-23) sets how far a deployed satellite's apogee, perigee and inclination may differ from what was notified, for non-geostationary systems under Res 35 with eccentricity below 0.5 and apogee below 15,000 km.

Difference from the filing (≤ 2,000 km)What the operator must do
Up to ±70 km, ±2° (resolves 6)Explain the difference
±70–100 km or 2–3° (resolves 7)Explain it and show it causes no extra interference
Beyond ±100 km or 3° (resolves 9)Modify the notification
After deployment: ±30 km (resolves 11)Stay inside; at most 60 consecutive days outside (resolves 12)

Above 2,000 km the altitude limits are ±5% / ±10% of the notified altitude and inclination ±3° / ±4°. Source: ITU WRS-24, Resolution 8 and 35.

How far reported orbits are from the filing

How to read this
  • Notified orbit: the orbital planes the administration filed with the ITU, as listed in the network's latest Res 35 milestone report. Each box is one planned satellite position in a plane.
  • Reported satellite (square with coloured edges): a satellite the operator listed as deployed in that same report, drawn on the apogee, perigee and inclination given for it there. Its colour says which Res 8 limit it is inside. These are the operator's own numbers at report time, not tracked orbits; tracked (actual) orbits can be added later as a third kind of marker.
  • RAAN and phasing are not in the filings. Planes with the same inclination and altitude (a shell) are spread evenly around the equator, and satellites evenly along each plane. The tolerance comparison does not depend on this.
  • Each reported satellite rides alongside one planned slot of its plane, so the gap between the coloured square and the grey box is only the difference in altitude and inclination.
  • The tolerance box is the cross-section across the direction of travel: radially from notified − limit to notified + limit altitude, sideways by ±Δi. Apogee and perigee limits are applied separately, so elliptical orbits get a box that changes along the orbit.
  • For the 2,000 km threshold the stricter inclination limit is used only if both notified apogee and perigee are at or below 2,000 km; the resolution's wording does not settle this case.
  • Satellites move eastward for inclinations below 90° (counter-clockwise seen from above the N pole). Most constellations here are near-polar, so they look like they travel north–south. The Earth is drawn without its rotation.
